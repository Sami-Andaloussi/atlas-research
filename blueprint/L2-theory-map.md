# L2 — Theory map

## TL;DR

A map of economic explanations instead of a house view: 72 named lenses in 23 families, most of them competing.
Each lens declares what it assumes, what it reads, where it fails, the simpler reading it must beat, and what would count against it.
Subject cards may only cite lenses from this map, so every reading in a report names the theory behind it.

## Purpose

Make the theory behind a reading **explicit, comparable and refutable**. A report that says
"policy is tight" is taking a theoretical position; Atlas makes it say which one, and keeps the
rival positions next to it.

**Success looks like:**
- Every lens a card cites exists in the map, with every field filled or a declared gap.
- For every central question (inflation, policy stance, the currency, the cycle), at least two
  lenses with different mechanisms are on the map.
- A reader can see, from the map alone, what each lens would need to see to be proved wrong.

## What a family declares

Each family states its definition, its role, the questions it serves (diagnosis, explanation,
forecast, scenario), its assumptions and required inputs, the kinds of output it may produce
(claim, state, scenario, formal model), and its **applicability**: when it applies, when it does
not, and what it does when a precondition fails. A monetary family facing a target with no
financing channel restricts itself to describing liquidity; it does not invent a transmission.

## What a lens declares

| Field | What it states |
|---|---|
| Role | What the lens explains, in one sentence. |
| Assumptions | What must be true for the reading to hold. |
| Inputs | The data it reads, which later become named series in the catalogue ([L1](L1-facts.md)). |
| Outputs | The kinds of claim and scenario it may produce. |
| Limitations | Where it is known to be weak. |
| Alternatives | The competing lenses, families or model types. |
| Counterexample | A situation, dated where possible, in which it fails. |
| Baseline | The simpler reading it must beat to be worth using. |
| Disconfirming outcomes | What would count against it. |
| Metrics and tests | How it would be evaluated. |
| Robustness checks | The variations its reading must survive. |
| Retirement rule | When to stop using it, or demote it to description. |
| References | The works it rests on, or a declared gap. |

## Four lenses in detail

Central-bank stance, the dollar and currency risk: four lenses where the map is deepest, quoted
from the catalogue. Each shows the shape every lens takes.

### Rule-based policy stance (Taylor-type)

- **Reads:** whether monetary policy is tight or loose, by comparing the policy rate with the rate
  a simple rule prescribes from inflation's gap to target and the output or unemployment gap.
- **Assumes:** the central bank reacts more than one for one to inflation (the Taylor principle);
  a neutral real rate and a target can be stated for the date; slack is estimated well enough in
  real time to bear a rule.
- **Fails when:** the rate is at its floor. In 2009 a Taylor-type rule with an output-gap weight
  of one prescribed roughly −1.5 to −3 %, a rate no central bank could set; the stance was carried
  by guidance and asset purchases, and a "too tight" reading said nothing.
- **Must beat:** the policy rate compared with its own recent average.
- **Counts against it:** the policy rate moving persistently away from every rule in the family,
  with no floor, financial-stability or supply-shock explanation.
- **Weak spot:** the prescription moves one for one with the assumed neutral rate and the gap
  estimate, both unobserved and revised; so it is tested on real-time vintages as well as on
  revised data.
- **Rests on:** Taylor (1993); Woodford (2003); Walsh, *Monetary Theory and Policy*; Blanchard (2017).

### Natural-rate (r\*) gap

- **Reads:** the stance as the gap between the real policy rate and the neutral real rate, and
  slow shifts in the whole level of rates as shifts in that neutral rate.
- **Assumes:** a neutral rate exists at the chosen horizon and moves slowly; it can be inferred
  with potential output (Laubach-Williams type) or bracketed by long real forward rates.
- **Fails when:** a fixed neutral rate is used. After 2008 the US real policy rate stayed one to
  two points below zero for eight years while core inflation stayed under 2 % in almost every
  month: consistent with a collapsed neutral rate, not with loose policy. A fixed 2 % neutral rate
  would have read the stance backwards.
- **Must beat:** the real policy rate against a constant 2 % neutral rate.
- **Counts against it:** a restrictive reading persistently followed by accelerating demand and
  inflation, or the reverse, across the model family.
- **Retires to a band-only statement** when the estimate's confidence band covers both signs of
  the gap.
- **Rests on:** Laubach and Williams (2003); Woodford (2003); Walsh, *Monetary Theory and Policy*.

### Global dollar-funding cycle

- **Reads:** world financial conditions, cross-border credit and risk-asset prices through the
  dollar's role as the dominant funding currency. A stronger dollar and tighter US policy shrink
  the balance sheets of non-US dollar borrowers and of the global banks that lend to them.
- **Inputs:** the broad dollar index, split against advanced and emerging currencies; the US
  policy path and two-year yield; financial-conditions indices; the cross-currency basis;
  emerging-market spreads and flows.
- **Fails when:** official dollar supply cuts the squeeze short. In March 2020 the broad dollar
  rose about 8 % in two weeks and peaked on 23 March as the Fed's balance sheet began a
  2.4-trillion expansion; conditions eased within weeks, yet the dollar gave back only part of its
  gain until mid-year. Dollar strength alone does not tell how long the tightening lasts.
- **Must beat:** a parity-based dollar valuation that ignores balance sheets and funding.
- **Counts against it:** sustained dollar appreciation with loosening non-US conditions and a
  narrowing basis, across several episodes.
- **Weak spot:** the dollar is both cause and symptom, since risk-off strengthens it as a safe
  haven; safe-haven episodes are separated from policy-driven moves using dated policy surprises.
- **Rests on:** Rey (2015); Bruno and Shin (2015); Avdjiev, Du, Koch and Shin (2019);
  Miranda-Agrippino and Rey (2020).

### Balance-sheet mismatch

- **Reads:** why a depreciation that should restore competitiveness produces a recession instead,
  when liabilities are in foreign currency and revenues are not.
- **Assumes:** a material share of borrowers owes foreign currency against local revenue; net worth
  constrains borrowing; currency composition is observable below the national aggregate.
- **Fails when:** a large real depreciation is followed by faster growth and falling
  non-performing loans, the expansionary case the lens denies.
- **Must beat:** a depreciation read as expansionary through the trade channel alone.
- **Counts against it:** investment by heavily foreign-currency-indebted firms falling no more than
  others' after a depreciation; insolvencies concentrating among borrowers with no foreign-currency
  exposure.
- **Weak spot:** hedges written off balance sheet are invisible to it, so exposure is measured gross
  as well as net of declared hedges.
- **Retires** when the currency mismatch closes in the target, which removes the channel it rests on.
- **Rests on:** Krugman (1999); Céspedes, Chang and Velasco (2004).

## Three readings of the same inflation

The inflation family shows why the map is a map. The same rise in prices, three serious
explanations, and what each would need to see to be wrong:

| Lens | Explains inflation through | What would count against it |
|---|---|---|
| New Keynesian sticky prices | staggered price setting and forward-looking expectations | expectations stay anchored, yet the lens misses a realised burst of inflation |
| Conflict and cost-push | cost shocks, defence of margins, wage-price bargaining | costs and margins rise, yet broad inflation does not propagate |
| Fiscal theory of the price level | the price level adjusting so that real government debt equals the present value of expected primary surpluses | a measured fall in expected future surpluses, with real yields unchanged, moves neither prices, nor expectations, nor the value of long bonds |

## Gates

**Entry.** The list of families is fixed, with a definition and applicability for each.

**Exit.** Pass when:
- every lens has every field filled, or a declared reference gap;
- every lens's inputs are mapped to series keys in the catalogue, or marked as not yet sourced;
- each central US subject (inflation, policy and rates, the dollar, the cycle) has at least two
  lenses with different mechanisms;
- an independent reviewer has checked the counterexamples and the disconfirming outcomes of the
  lenses the first cards cite.

## Steps

1. **Families.** *Design.* Definition, role, applicability and failure behaviour of each family.
2. **Lenses.** *Design.* Each lens with its fields, written from its references, never from memory.
3. **Thin families.** *Design.* Deepen the families a first subject needs, as far as the subject
   demands: two lenses is a floor, never a ceiling.
4. **Inputs to series.** *Execution.* Map each lens's inputs to series keys of the catalogue.
5. **Review.** *Design.* An independent review of the economic content, starting with the lenses
   the first cards cite.

## Deliverables

- The theory catalogue, one file per family, versioned.
- The map of lens inputs to series keys.
- The list of declared gaps: references missing, inputs not yet sourced.

## What it refuses

| It refuses | Because |
|---|---|
| A lens without a counterexample or a disconfirming outcome | A lens that cannot fail cannot be scored. |
| A lens without a baseline | A reading that does not beat the obvious one adds nothing. |
| A reference cited from memory | Every reference is read and located before it is cited. |
| A card citing a lens that is not on the map | Every reading in a report must name its theory. |
