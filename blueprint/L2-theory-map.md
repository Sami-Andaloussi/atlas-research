# L2 — Theory map

## TL;DR

A map of economic explanations instead of a single house view: 72 named lenses in 23 families, most of them competing.
Each lens declares what it assumes, what it reads, where it fails, the simpler reading it must beat, and what would count against it.
Subject cards may only cite lenses from this map, so every reading in a report names the theory behind it.

## Purpose

Make the theory behind a reading **explicit, comparable and refutable**. A report that says
"policy is tight" is taking a theoretical position; Atlas makes it say which one, and keeps the
rival positions next to it.

**Success looks like:**
- Every lens a card cites exists in the map, with every field filled or a declared gap.
- For every central question (inflation, policy stance, the currency, the cycle), at least two
  lenses with different mechanisms are on the map.
- A reader can see, from the map alone, what each lens would need to see to be proved wrong.

**Not every entry is a rival theory.** Some are frameworks used alongside the others rather than
against them: the stochastic discount factor and no-arbitrage pricing, expected utility, and the
three accounting identities. The parity anchor reads currencies through interest-rate and inflation
differentials (interest parity, purchasing-power parity).

**The counts are uneven, and the table shows it on purpose.** Choosing which explanations are on
the map is itself a choice; publishing the counts lets a reader see where the map is thin. A
question is not confined to one family: a question about the Fed can also draw on the business
cycle, inflation and fiscal lenses.

The map grows with the cards. The Swiss and euro-area cards will need lenses it does not yet hold,
such as the franc as a safe haven and exchange-rate intervention; the workshop adds them, with every
field, before those cards are written ([build sequence](build-sequence.md)).

## What a family declares

Each family states its definition, its role, the questions it serves (diagnosis, explanation,
forecast, scenario), its assumptions and required inputs, the kinds of output it may produce
(claim, state, scenario, formal model), and its **applicability**: when it applies, when it does
not, and what it does when a precondition fails. A monetary family facing a target with no
financing channel restricts itself to describing liquidity; it does not invent a transmission.

## What a lens declares

| Field | What it states |
|---|---|
| Role | What the lens explains, in one sentence. |
| Assumptions | What must be true for the reading to hold. |
| Inputs | The data it reads, which later become named series in the catalogue ([L1](L1-facts.md)). |
| Outputs | The kinds of claim and scenario it may produce. |
| Limitations | Where it is known to be weak. |
| Alternatives | The competing lenses, families or model types. |
| Counterexample | A situation, dated where possible, in which it fails. |
| Baseline | The simpler reading it must beat to be worth using. |
| Disconfirming outcomes | What would count against it. |
| Metrics and tests | How it would be evaluated. |
| Robustness checks | The variations its reading must survive. |
| Retirement rule | When to stop using it, or demote it to description. |
| References | The works it rests on, or a declared gap. |

## Four lenses in detail

Central-bank stance, the dollar and currency risk, with the fields every lens declares. The
contents come from the catalogue; the counterexamples are dated so that they can be checked.

### Rule-based policy stance (Taylor-type)

- **Role:** read whether monetary policy is tight or loose by comparing the policy rate with the
  rate a simple rule prescribes from inflation's gap to target and the output or unemployment gap.
- **Assumptions:** the central bank reacts more than one for one to inflation (the Taylor
  principle); a neutral real rate and a target can be stated for the date; slack is estimated well
  enough in real time to bear a rule.
- **Inputs:** the policy rate; core PCE inflation against the target in force; the output gap or the
  unemployment gap; an assumed neutral real rate.
- **Counterexample:** at the zero lower bound. In 2009, rules in the family prescribed rates from
  about zero (Taylor's 1993 weights) to several points below zero (rules with a larger weight on
  slack), rates no central bank could set. The stance was then carried by guidance and asset
  purchases, and the rule's gap is read against a shadow rate rather than the policy rate.
- **Baseline:** the policy rate compared with its own recent average.
- **Disconfirming outcomes:** the reading is judged by what follows it. A "restrictive" reading that
  is followed, over the next four to eight quarters, by demand and inflation no weaker than a
  no-change benchmark counts against it; so does a "loose" reading followed by no pick-up.
- **Limitation and robustness:** the prescription moves one for one with the assumed neutral rate
  and the gap estimate, both unobserved and revised. The reading is run across a fixed family of
  rules, on headline and core inflation, and on real-time vintages as well as revised data; the
  spread of prescriptions is published as its uncertainty.
- **Retirement:** demoted to description at the lower bound, or under a framework that is not a
  reaction to inflation and slack.
- **References:** Taylor (1993); Woodford (2003); Walsh, *Monetary Theory and Policy*; Blanchard
  (2017).

### Natural-rate (r\*) gap

- **Role:** read the stance as the gap between the real policy rate and the neutral real rate, and
  slow shifts in the whole level of rates as shifts in that neutral rate.
- **Assumptions:** a neutral rate exists at the chosen horizon and moves slowly; it can be inferred
  with potential output (Laubach-Williams type) or bracketed by long real forward rates.
- **Inputs:** the policy rate less expected or core inflation; published estimates of the neutral
  rate with their bands; long real forward rates from inflation-indexed bonds; the output gap.
- **Counterexample:** the reading a fixed neutral rate gives. From 2009 to 2016 the US real policy
  rate, measured with core PCE, stayed below zero while core inflation stayed under 2 % in almost
  every month: consistent with a collapsed neutral rate, not with loose policy. A constant 2 %
  neutral rate reads that stance backwards, which is why the lens reads the published estimate.
- **Baseline:** the real policy rate against a constant 2 % neutral rate.
- **Disconfirming outcomes:** a restrictive reading persistently followed by accelerating demand and
  inflation, or the reverse, across the estimation methods.
- **Limitation and robustness:** estimates are imprecise and revised as new data arrive, and the
  market forward rate mixes the neutral rate with a term premium. The gap is always published with
  its band, from at least two methods, on real-time as well as smoothed estimates; a band that
  covers both signs is a normal output, "stance indeterminate", not a failure.
- **References:** Laubach and Williams (2003); Woodford (2003); Walsh, *Monetary Theory and Policy*.

### Global dollar-funding cycle

- **Role:** explain world financial conditions, cross-border credit and risk-asset prices through the
  dollar's role as the dominant funding currency. A stronger dollar and tighter US policy shrink the
  balance sheets of non-US dollar borrowers and of the global banks that lend to them.
- **Assumptions:** a large stock of dollar debt is owed by non-US borrowers whose revenues are not in
  dollars; global banks' capacity to lend dollars moves with the dollar's value; US policy moves a
  common global factor in risky assets that floating exchange rates only partly insulate.
- **Inputs:** the Fed's broad dollar index, against advanced and emerging currencies; the US policy
  path and two-year yield; financial-conditions indices; the cross-currency basis and
  emerging-market spreads where a public source exists.
- **Counterexample:** March 2020. The broad dollar index rose about 8 % in two weeks to a peak on
  23 March. The Fed widened its dollar swap lines with other central banks on 15 and 19 March and
  opened a repo facility for foreign central banks on 31 March; funding conditions eased within
  weeks, yet the dollar gave back only part of its gain until mid-year. Official dollar supply can
  cut a squeeze short, so dollar strength alone does not tell how long the tightening lasts.
- **Baseline:** a parity-based dollar valuation that ignores balance sheets and funding.
- **Disconfirming outcomes:** sustained dollar appreciation with loosening non-US financial
  conditions and a narrowing basis, across several episodes.
- **Limitation and robustness:** the dollar is both cause and symptom, since risk-off strengthens it
  as a safe haven. Safe-haven episodes are separated from policy-driven moves with series of
  monetary-policy surprises, each read as of its own publication date.
- **References:** Rey (2015); Bruno and Shin (2015); Avdjiev, Du, Koch and Shin (2019);
  Miranda-Agrippino and Rey (2020).

### Balance-sheet mismatch

- **Role:** explain why a depreciation that should restore competitiveness produces a recession
  instead, when liabilities are in foreign currency and revenues are not.
- **Assumptions:** a material share of borrowers owes foreign currency against local revenue; net
  worth constrains borrowing; currency composition is observable below the national aggregate.
- **Inputs:** liabilities by currency and sector; revenue exposure to tradable prices; credit growth
  and non-performing loans after the depreciation; investment by leverage and currency exposure.
- **Counterexample:** a large real depreciation followed by faster growth and falling non-performing
  loans, the expansionary case the lens denies.
- **Baseline:** a depreciation read as expansionary through the trade channel alone.
- **Disconfirming outcomes:** investment by heavily foreign-currency-indebted firms falling no more
  than others' after a depreciation; insolvencies concentrating among borrowers with no
  foreign-currency exposure.
- **Limitation and robustness:** hedges written off balance sheet are invisible to it, so exposure
  is measured gross and net of declared hedges, with firms matched on sector and size.
- **Retirement:** when the currency mismatch closes in the target, which removes the channel.
- **References:** Krugman (1999); Céspedes, Chang and Velasco (2004).

## Three readings of the same inflation

The inflation family shows why the map is a map. The same rise in prices, three serious
explanations, and what each would need to see to be wrong:

| Lens | Explains inflation through | What would count against it |
|---|---|---|
| New Keynesian sticky prices | expected inflation and real marginal cost, or slack, with staggered price setting as the friction that makes inflation persist | expectations stay anchored and slack is stable, yet a burst of inflation is realised |
| Conflict and cost-push | cost shocks, defence of margins, wage-price bargaining | costs and margins rise, yet broad inflation does not propagate |
| Fiscal theory of the price level | the price level adjusting so that real government debt equals the present value of expected primary surpluses | a measured fall in expected future surpluses, with real yields unchanged, moves neither prices, nor expectations, nor the value of long bonds. Expected surpluses are not observed directly, so this lens is weakly identified and is used to describe, not to score |

## Gates

**Entry.** The list of families is fixed, with a definition and applicability for each.

**Exit.** Pass when:
- every lens has every field filled, or a declared reference gap;
- every lens's inputs are mapped to series keys in the catalogue, or marked as not yet sourced;
- each central US subject (inflation, policy and rates, the dollar, the cycle) has at least two
  lenses with different mechanisms;
- an independent reviewer has checked the counterexamples and the disconfirming outcomes of the
  lenses the first cards cite.

## Steps

1. **Families.** *Design.* Definition, role, applicability and failure behaviour of each family.
2. **Lenses.** *Design.* Each lens with its fields, written from its references, never from memory.
3. **Thin families.** *Design.* Deepen the families a first subject needs, as far as the subject
   demands: two lenses is a floor, never a ceiling.
4. **Inputs to series.** *Execution.* Map each lens's inputs to series keys of the catalogue.
5. **Review.** *Design.* An independent review of the economic content, starting with the lenses
   the first cards cite.

## Deliverables

- The theory catalogue, one file per family, versioned.
- The map of lens inputs to series keys.
- The list of declared gaps: references missing, inputs not yet sourced.

## What it refuses

| It refuses | Because |
|---|---|
| A lens without a counterexample or a disconfirming outcome | A lens that cannot fail cannot be scored. |
| A lens without a baseline | A reading that does not beat the obvious one adds nothing. |
| A reference cited from memory | Every reference is read and located before it is cited. |
| A card citing a lens that is not on the map | Every reading in a report must name its theory. |
